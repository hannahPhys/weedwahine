How Mary Jane interacts with your menstrual cycle

February 6th, 2021

While scientists have been studying the effects of cannabis on the human (and animal) brain for decades, focusing on the differences in effect between the sexes is a relatively new phenomenon. It may surprise you to find out that cannabis not only affects men and women differently, but also affects the female brain differently at various points in a woman’s cycle. Many of the differences are likely caused by sex hormones, particularly the presence of estrogen, meaning lady tokers metabolize weed differently at different points in their life cycles (pre versus post-menopause for example) as well as their monthly cycles. Differences may also be caused by variations in body fat distribution and muscle mass between men and women, making the issue of how marijuana affects women a complicated one!

In the early ’90s scientists discovered that humans have what is called an endocannabinoid system, and that system is what interacts with cannabinoids when we consume the still-controversial cannabis plant. Said plant has more than 110 cannabinoids, some of which you may have heard of—like THC (aka, tetrahydrocannabinol, the compound that gives you a head euphoria) and CBD (which doesn’t have psychoactive effects, but produces a body euphoria). The endocannabinoid system aims to regulate other key systems from stress:

  • Nervous system - neurotransmitters like serotonin, GABA, and dopamine
  • Immune system - inflammatory response agents like microglia and cytokines
  • Endocrine system - both steroidal (estrogen) and metabolic

It is believed that we have more cannabinoid receptors in our body than men—and that those receptors are centered around our reproductive organs—which could explain why women have reported success using cannabis to aid in period pain, endometriosis and the like.

There are known differences in the function of the endocannabinoid system between ladies and guys, which may be one reason that women experience cannabis differently than men. CB1 receptor density changes in response to the presence of certain sex hormones, meaning that the ability of cannabinoids like THC to be absorbed and processed by the body fluctuates depending on hormone levels.

This means that women tend to become sensitized to the effects of THC more quickly than fellas which is a good ego boost when you can out-smoke all the guys at the sesh. But also this means we have to keep in mind, how much you are smoking and how regularly.

Sex hormones don’t just affect the number of brain receptors—certain hormones have a direct effect on the chemicals in cannabis. Estrogen is the most notable of these hormones because it regulates the production of fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH), a chemical that degrades the endocannabinoid anandamide, which is the human body’s natural version of THC. When estrogen levels are decreased, FAAH is less regulated and can freely degrade anandamide, resulting in greater anxiety and depression and a reduced response to THC.

The graph above shows the estrogen level over an average menstrual cycle. When estrogen levels climb it does 2 things:

  1. Creates more cannabinoid receptors
  2. Suppresses the FAAH enzyme that breaks down cannabinoids

These actions makes you feel the effects of cannabinoids stronger and longer. During ovulation our estrogen levels peak which means we would experience a heightened response to THC and we will also feel at our best.

After ovulation when your estrogen levels are decreasing you start to experiencing cravings for carbs, chocolate, ice cream and also experience higher cravings for weed. Of course this isn't a bad thing. Cannabis has been proven over and over again to help women who suffer from period cramps, breast pain, and endometriosis.
